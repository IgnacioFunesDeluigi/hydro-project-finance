# Project Charter: Hydro Project Finance

## Question
- If a river's flow is less reliable than the bank assumes, how much less debt can a hydroelectric plant take on?
- How much senior debt can a run-of-river power plant of approximately 10 MW on an Argentine river (with actual daily flow records) sustain, when sized based on a minimum DSCR calculated against P90 energy production; and what is the probability of breaching the DSCR covenant when historical hydrological variability is simulated?

## Hypotheses
- H1: Debt capacity is determined by dry years in particular, by sequences of several consecutive dry years.
- H2: The design storm method alters the CAPEX for the tailings storage facility and the losses in the tailings, but has little impact on the debt sized at the P90 level.
- H3: If the years are modeled as independent (ignoring the fact that a dry year often follows another), the probability of breaching the covenant is underestimated. 

## In scope
- One hypothetical run-of-river plant of about 10 MW on an Argentine river with at least 30 years of daily streamflow records.
- Hydrology module (Python): data cleaning, flow-duration curve, design flow, environmental flow, annual energy series, P50 and P90 energy.
- Financial model (Excel): semi-annual timeline, sources and uses, CFADS, debt sculpted to a target DSCR on P90 energy, debt service reserve account, cash flow waterfall, DSCR, LLCR and equity IRR, sensitivities and a checks sheet.
- Monte Carlo simulation (Python): block bootstrap of historical years, distribution of the minimum DSCR, probability of breaching lock-up and default covenants, convergence tests, reconciliation with the Excel base case.
- v2: flood risk module using the three design-storm methods from my thesis, and Argentina-specific scenarios (payment delays, FX shock, curtailment).
- v3 (if time allows): a generative model of streamflow series built from scratch, benchmarked against the bootstrap with out-of-sample validation.


## Out of scope
- Real project data from an existing plant: the plant is hypothetical, built on public data and published cost benchmarks.
- Engineering design of the plant (civil works, turbine selection): only the parameters the financial model needs.
- Legal documentation, contracts and guarantees: stated as assumptions, not modeled.
- Detailed Argentine tax law: simplified income tax only.
- Investment advice: the model answers a research question, not whether to invest.


## Deliverables and dates

## Data sources
- Daily streamflow: Sistema Nacional de Informacion Hidrica (SNIH), Argentina. Station to be selected in phase 0.
- Capital costs: IRENA, Renewable Power Generation Costs in 2024.
- Electricity price: private PPAs under the MATER market (range to be verified).
- Financing terms (tenor, margins, DSCR targets, reserve sizing): published project finance references, each one cited in DECISIONS.md.
- Synthetic storm events (v2): output generated by my thesis director, Ing. Carlos Catalini, credited as his work.

## Success criteria
- The model answers the research question with one number: how much less debt the plant supports at P90 than at P50, and the probability of a covenant breach.
- All checks in the Excel model equal zero.
- The Python base case matches the Excel model within 0.1%.
- Monte Carlo results remain stable when the number of simulations or the random seed changes.
- I can explain every formula and assumption without notes.
- At least one external reviewer (hydrology and project finance) has reviewed the work before v1.0.
